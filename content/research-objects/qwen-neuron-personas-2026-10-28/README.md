# Qwen neuron–persona steering: a source-attributed research object

**Evidence cut:** 2026-10-28. **Status:** reported findings, not our own GPU replication. **License:** MIT for this original transcription; all upstream sources retain their own rights.

This is the existing AI Research Notebook's public, data-only source contribution, not an independent research platform or newly invented skill. It applies the 561 Group's established Research Fabrication discipline: observations → supported claims → source evidence → open questions → reproducible object → independently received publication. The generic schema is at [experiment-evidence-v1.schema.json](../experiment-evidence-v1.schema.json). The companion [evidence.json](evidence.json) assigns a model, study design, source, epistemic status, unit, denominator (where relevant), and caveat to every reported figure.

**The finding:** Selected runtime perturbations in **Qwen3.5-4B** can shift model responses toward hostility or sycophancy in particular prompts, while an independent **Qwen3-8B** atlas reports 34,220 automatically persona-classified replies among 884,736 neuron–direction tests. These are different models, experiments and denominators. Neither proves that 34,220 enduring personalities, conscious selves or intentions exist inside a network.

The research object is suitable for programmatic downstream use with standard JSON Schema Draft 2020-12 validation. Schema validity is **not** experimental replication: an independent receiver must inspect original reports, acquired responses, selection controls, natural-activation doses, batch layouts, external model labels and accuracy effects before upgrading any claim.

**2026-10-29 source reconciliation — [AI Research Notebook issue #3](https://github.com/lenticule-science/artificial-intelligence-research-notebook-science-services-section/issues/3):** The original [Watson research note](https://quasiqualia.com/notes/lyra-neurons.html), section **03 · Results**, explicitly supports the packet's steered MMLU range **27–71%** against an unsteered **73.7%**. The four **trait-replicating** neurons are a narrower subset: L14N8949 **45%**, L12N2920 **47%**, L16N8168 **39%**, and L10N6295 **27%**. Their highest MMLU result is 47%, not 71%; the wider upper bound belongs to the study's eight studied neurons, including interventions that did not replicate their requested persona traits. Watson describes 300 forced-choice MMLU items per neuron and dose-matched random-neuron controls. This resolves a **source-population ambiguity**, not a new run, a changed Watson result, or a reproduction of any underlying model outputs. The original 2026-10-28 evidence cut and source-attributed epistemic status remain unchanged; `evidence.json` records the qualified ranges.
